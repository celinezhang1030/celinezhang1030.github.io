Rowing · 25 photos · 14 videos

✦

I was in sixth grade when I first picked up an oar, and somehow, I already felt that rowing was meant for me. Even during those early sessions on indoor rowing machines and in the training tank—where repetition could make the hours feel endless—I was drawn to the sport in a way I couldn’t quite explain.

Then came the water. The wind against my face, the rhythm of each stroke, the quiet expanse stretching ahead of me. Out there, I found a rare kind of freedom: no need to be anything other than fully present.

Soon, rowing became a defining part of my life. I joined my district’s rowing team, trained through afternoons, weekends, and school breaks, and eventually earned China’s National Level II Athlete certification. But in ninth grade, a serious lower-back injury forced me off the water for more than a year.

Returning taught me something that years of pushing harder never had: strength is not about ignoring your limits. It is about rebuilding patiently, listening to your body, and finding the courage to begin again. Today, I still row with the same love that first drew me to the water—only now, I know that taking care of myself is part of the journey, too.
