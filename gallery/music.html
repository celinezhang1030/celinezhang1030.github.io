My mother first noticed my musical ear when I was four: I could hear a melody and find its notes on the piano without being taught. Music seemed to find me everywhere. As a child, I would sing or dance whenever I passed a place filled with music.

Piano practice, however, was not always so enchanting. By third grade, I was ready to quit. My mother made me a deal: if I went a whole year without practicing, she would sell the piano. Somewhere along the way, I realized I didn't want to give music up after all. I kept playing, this time because I wanted to.

Chopin is my favorite companion at the piano. I love the emotional intensity of his music, from the restless brilliance of Fantaisie-Impromptu to the intimacy of his nocturnes. Yet music has never been a solo experience for me. As principal flute in my school orchestras, a performer in school musicals, and later a member of my high school choir, I discovered the joy of listening, responding, and creating something together.

Whether I am playing a piano passage or blending my voice into a choir, music reminds me that the most beautiful things are not always made alone.
